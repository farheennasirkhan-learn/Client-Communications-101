Section 01 · The real skill

Controlling the narrative

Scene · Week 4 — GenAI deployment, US logistics company
An FDE has hit a real blocker. The client's internal data pipeline is producing malformed JSON that's breaking the retrieval layer. The FDE knows about it. They've been working on a fix for two days. They haven't mentioned it to the client because they wanted to resolve it first before "worrying anyone." On Friday, the client's VP of Technology joins the weekly call and asks why the demo environment is still down. The FDE is caught off guard. The VP isn't upset about the bug. He's upset that he found out this way.
That story is a communication failure.
The technical work was right
The FDE did everything right technically: they identified the issue, were fixing it, and had a plan.
The narrative was lost
They lost the client's confidence because they didn't control the narrative.
Client communication isn't about being polite, sending timely updates, or formatting emails correctly, though all of that matters. It's about making sure the client always knows where things stand, what the risks are, and what's being done about them. Before they have to ask.
The standard every FDE is held to

If a client is surprised, it's a communication failure — regardless of whether the technical work was excellent.

Everything in this session is in service of meeting this standard.
